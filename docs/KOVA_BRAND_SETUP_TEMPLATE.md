# Kova Agent — Brand Setup Template

### The Complete Brand Onboarding Worksheet

**Use this template for every business you set up on Kova. Fill it out thoroughly — every field you complete makes Kova's AI agents smarter about the brand. Skip nothing.**

---

## How to Use This Template

1. **Fill out every section** with the business owner (or do your own research)
2. **Be specific, not generic** — "professionals aged 25-45" is weak. "First-time startup founders in Nairobi, aged 25-35, technical background, active on Twitter, frustrated by marketing" is strong.
3. **Use real language** — Write the brand voice the way you'd actually talk to customers, not how a textbook says to
4. **The examples matter most** — The AI learns more from 3 real example posts than from a paragraph of description
5. Once complete, transfer each section into the corresponding Kova fields during account setup

## Section 2: Brand Voice — The Most Critical Section

> *This is the single most important field in Kova. Every piece of content the Create Agent writes is shaped by this. Spend real time here.*

### Brand Voice Description

Write 3-5 sentences that describe HOW this brand speaks. Not WHAT it says — HOW it says it.

Think about:
- Is it formal or casual?
- Does it use slang, jargon, or keep it clean?
- Is it funny, serious, inspirational, provocative?
- Does it speak as "we", "I", or third person?
- What language(s) does it use? (English? Swahili? Sheng? Mix?)
- Who does it sound like? (A knowledgeable friend? A strict expert? A hype person?)
- What would it NEVER say?

```
[Write 3-5 sentences here]
```

**Good example (Unimart Africa):**
> "Young, vibrant, and relatable. We speak like a smart friend who gets campus life. We use casual but respectful language, occasional slang (campus vibes, hustle, squad), and emojis. We champion student entrepreneurship, peer-to-peer commerce, and financial empowerment. Our tone is encouraging, slightly playful, and always inclusive. We mix English with occasional Swahili/Sheng phrases when targeting East African campuses."

**Good example (Law firm):**
> "Authoritative but approachable. We never talk down to clients. We explain complex legal concepts using everyday language and real-life examples. Our tone is calm, confident, and reassuring — like a trusted advisor, not a courtroom lawyer. We use 'we' and address readers as 'you'. No legal jargon without explanation. No exclamation marks or emojis. We are serious about our work but warm with our people."

**Good example (Street food brand):**
> "Loud, proud, and unapologetically delicious. We talk like we're shouting across a busy market in the best way possible. Heavy on emojis 🔥🍗, informal grammar (coz, lowkey, no cap), and food slang. We hype every dish like it's the main character. We roast competitors playfully and gas up our customers. Energy: your loudest friend who also happens to be an incredible cook."

**Bad example (too generic):**
> "Professional and friendly. We aim to be informative and helpful." ← This tells the AI nothing. Every brand says this.

## Section 4: Content Pillars — What Do We Talk About?

> *Content pillars are the 4-6 recurring themes that make up your content strategy. Everything the Create Agent produces will fall under one of these.*

### How to Choose Pillars

Each pillar should:
- Be broad enough to generate dozens of post ideas
- Be relevant to your audience's interests (not just what you want to sell)
- Balance between promotional (buy our stuff) and valuable (here's something useful)
- Cover different parts of the customer journey (awareness → consideration → conversion → loyalty)

### The Pillar Framework

| Pillar Type | Purpose | Example |
|------------|---------|---------|
| **Pillar 1: Core Value** | What you sell/do, directly | "Our Products/Services" |
| **Pillar 2: Expertise** | Teaching, tips, industry knowledge | "Industry Tips & How-Tos" |
| **Pillar 3: Culture/Lifestyle** | Connecting with your audience's identity | "Campus Life", "Startup Culture" |
| **Pillar 4: Social Proof** | Testimonials, case studies, wins | "Customer Success Stories" |
| **Pillar 5: Behind the Scenes** | Humanizing the brand | "Our Process", "Meet the Team" |
| **Pillar 6: Community** | Partnerships, events, user spotlights | "Our Community", "Events" |

## Section 15: Brand Setup Quality Scorecard

After completing the template, score yourself:

| Section | Excellent (3) | Good (2) | Weak (1) | Score |
|---------|-------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Brand Voice | 5+ sentences, specific, with personality | 2-3 sentences, somewhat generic | 1 sentence or copy-pasted | /3 |
| Voice Examples | 3 diverse, authentic-sounding examples | 2 examples, decent quality | 1 or generic examples | /3 |
| Target Audience | Paragraph with age, location, psychographics | Basic demographics only | "Everyone" or 1 line | /3 |
| Content Pillars | 4-6 with descriptions and mix % | 3-4 without descriptions | Less than 3 or vague | /3 |
| Goals | 3-5 specific, measurable goals | 2-3 general goals | 1 vague goal | /3 |
| Business Deep Dive | Origin story + stats + testimonials + FAQs | Some context filled in | Mostly empty | /3 |
| Competitor Info | 3+ competitors with handles and analysis | 1-2 competitors listed | None | /3 |
| First 10 Seeds | All 10 with detailed notes | 5-7 seeds | Less than 5 | /3 |
| Visual Guidelines | Colors + style + emoji + hashtag strategy | Some visual guidelines | None | /3 |
| Guardrails | 5+ never-do rules + sensitive topics | A few boundaries | None | /3 |
| **TOTAL** | | | | **/30** |

**Scoring:**
- **25-30:** Kova will produce excellent, on-brand content from day 1
