## Issue, Solution & Beneficiaries (350 words or less)

Across Africa, millions of small businesses know they need social media to survive—but they cannot keep up. Marketing eats ten to fifteen hours a week when owners are already the accountant, the salesperson, and the delivery person. Hiring an agency costs far more than most SMEs earn in a month. Western tools like Buffer or Hootsuite assume you have time, a laptop, a credit card, and someone to write every post. The result is predictable: inconsistent posting, lost leads, and businesses that stay invisible online even when their product is good.

I lived this. At Africa Impact Academy in Machakos, I watched a founder run production, sales, and operations while her social media sat silent. With Unimart Africa, I was the same person—building the product by day and guilt-tripping myself about Instagram at midnight. If I struggle as a data science student who can code, what chance does a chapati seller in Nairobi or a fashion designer in Kakuma have?

Kova Agent tackles this directly. It is an AI marketing operating system built in Nairobi for how African businesses actually work. Six agents research your market, draft platform-native content, schedule it, publish it, and help you engage customers. You open your Daily Brief each morning, approve posts in Studio in about five minutes, and move on with your day. Beyond content, Kova connects selling to the channels Africans use: WhatsApp ordering, M-Pesa checkout, public shop pages, and lead capture from QR codes and walk-ins. Pricing starts around KES 499 per month—not agency fees, not dollar-priced Silicon Valley software.

Our beneficiaries are African SME owners—especially women-led businesses, youth founders, and informal traders moving online for the first time. Campus entrepreneurs like my Unimart sellers are an early segment. When these businesses grow online, the impact ripples outward: owners earn more, employees keep jobs, and local customers discover products they would never have found. Kova is not a one-time training workshop. It is daily infrastructure that works while founders sleep.

## Venture Model (500 words or less)

Kova Agent is a subscription-based AI marketing operating system for African small and medium enterprises. The model is simple: replace a fragmented, expensive marketing department with six AI agents and a five-minute daily approval ritual.

When a business signs up, the owner connects social accounts and completes a one-time brand profile—industry, voice, offerings, location, and goals. From that point, Kova works continuously. Six agents divide the labor: market research and trend monitoring, content creation tailored to each platform, scheduling at optimal times, publishing, engagement support, and learning from performance data through a Content DNA system that improves with every post.

Each morning the owner opens the Daily Brief—a summary of what happened, what is scheduled, and what needs attention. In Studio, they review AI-drafted posts, edit if needed, approve or reject, and move on. The goal is not more time in the app; it is less time on marketing overall. Approvals take about five minutes, not hours.

Revenue comes from monthly SaaS subscriptions tiered for African budgets—Starter for businesses getting online, Growth for those scaling engagement and WhatsApp, Pro for full multi-platform operations. We offer a free trial to reduce adoption risk. This is how the venture sustains itself long after any grant ends.

Beyond content, Kova closes the loop from attention to money. Public shop pages let businesses sell directly. M-Pesa integration lets customers pay on mobile—the dominant payment method in Kenya. WhatsApp ordering connects to how African customers already buy. Lead capture from QR codes, walk-ins, and messages ensures every buyer can become a contact in the pipeline.

Operationally, I built the platform as a web application deployed for production use. Unimart Africa—my campus e-commerce venture—is the first live customer, which means every feature exists because I needed it first. New SME customers go through onboarding, connect accounts, and enter a pilot period where we track posts published, leads captured, and sales facilitated.

The venture is for-profit because sustainability matters. A tool that disappears when donor funding ends fails the businesses we serve. Subscriptions align our success with customer success: if Kova does not help them grow, they cancel. That keeps us honest.

Over the next year, the model scales by replicating successful Nairobi pilots, publishing case studies, and refining onboarding so non-technical owners succeed without training manuals. Kova is infrastructure—used weekly, paid monthly, improved continuously.

## Risks & Challenges (500 words or less)

Every venture faces risks. I prefer to name mine honestly and show how I plan to respond.

**Financial risk** is the most immediate. Customer acquisition may be slower than product development. SMEs are cautious spenders, and subscription revenue takes time to compound. Mitigation: keep burn low (I am the primary builder), price accessibly with a free trial, focus on tight Nairobi pilots rather than broad marketing, and use Resolution funding for onboarding costs—not unsustainable subsidies.

**Operational risk** sits in being a solo founder. I code, sell, support, and study. Burnout or graduation could disrupt momentum. Mitigation: document systems and code thoroughly, prioritize features customers pay for over nice-to-haves, recruit one committed co-founder or core teammate by mid-2027, and use Resolution mentorship to decide when to hire first sales or support help.

**Technical risk** includes dependency on third-party APIs (social platforms, payment gateways, AI providers). Platforms change rules; APIs break; costs can shift. Mitigation: modular architecture so one integration failure does not collapse the product, monitor usage costs closely, and maintain human-in-the-loop approval so AI errors do not publish without owner consent.

**Stakeholder trust risk** matters for AI adoption. Some owners fear AI will misrepresent their brand or sound foreign. Mitigation: owner approves every post before publishing, brand voice training from their own inputs, and case studies showing real businesses with authentic results—not generic robot captions.

**Market risk:** target users may lack reliable internet or smartphones suited for complex apps. Mitigation: mobile-first design, lightweight pages, WhatsApp as a familiar channel, and shop links that work on basic smartphones.

**Regulatory and data privacy risk** grows as we handle customer and buyer data. Mitigation: seek legal guidance on Kenya data protection requirements, minimize data collection, secure payments through established M-Pesa flows, and be transparent with users about what Kova stores and why.

**Decision-making risk** as sole founder means blind spots. Mitigation: ally at USIU, Resolution mentors, customer feedback loops after every pilot, and willingness to kill features that do not drive outcomes.

I do not expect a risk-free path. I expect a builder's path—identify, adapt, ship again.
